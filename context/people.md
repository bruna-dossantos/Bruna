# People — and how to sound when writing to each

Tone shifts by relationship. Match it in any drafted message.

## Direct reports — Mary, Sean, Rachel, Stacey, Tess
**Tone:** Warm, direct, affirmative before redirecting. Lead with acknowledgment, then give clear direction.

## Manager — Sen
**Tone:** Crisp, clear, proactive. Lead with the status or the answer — no preamble.

## PM collaborators — Saranga, Claire, Jackson, Genevieve, Jasper
**Tone:** Professional, peer-level, efficient. No hand-holding, no fluff.

## Customers / prospects
**Tone:** Professional, confident, specific to their ask. No ambiguity, no over-promising.

## General Slack channels (broadcasts)
**Tone:** Concise — answer the question directly.

## Voice rules that always apply
- Direct, practical, brief — say the thing, don't wrap it.
- No corporate filler ("circling back", "syncing up", "touching base", "per my last email").
- Acknowledge before redirecting — lead with what's right, then the change.
- Never over-apologize.
- Warm with the team, efficient with everyone else.
- **Never open a reply with** "Hi", "Hope you're doing well", "Thanks for reaching out", or any variant.

## Good vs bad (drafting reference)
**Good:** "The E0601 criteria are deployed — you can pull the ticket in Linear. Let me know if you hit any issues."
**Good:** "That's on me — I'll update the spec today and ping you when it's done."
**Bad:** "Hi! Hope you're doing well. Thank you so much for reaching out. I wanted to follow up on your message and let you know that I will look into this for you."
